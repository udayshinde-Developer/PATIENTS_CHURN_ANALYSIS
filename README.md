# PATIENTS_CHURN_ANALYSIS
# 🏥 Patient Churn Analysis – Key Insights #

🔹 Analyzed **500 patients**, identifying **124 churned patients** and an overall **churn rate of 24.8%**, while **75.2% of patients remained active**, indicating relatively strong patient retention and engagement with healthcare services.

🔹 The patient population is almost evenly distributed by gender, with **Female patients accounting for 50.27%** and **Male patients for 49.73%**. This balanced distribution suggests that gender has little to no significant impact on patient churn behavior.

🔹 Patients with chronic diseases recorded **56 churned patients and 184 active patients**, while patients without chronic diseases recorded **68 churned patients and 192 active patients**. The similar retention patterns indicate that chronic disease status is not a major factor driving patient churn.

🔹 Insurance type appears to influence retention outcomes. **Government-insured patients recorded the highest number of churned patients (46)**, followed by **patients without insurance (41)** and those with **private insurance (37)**, highlighting the importance of insurance-related factors in patient retention.

🔹 The average patient satisfaction score is **3.05 out of 5**, and satisfaction analysis shows that **33.06% of churned patients belong to the lowest satisfaction group (1–2)**. This suggests that lower satisfaction levels are strongly associated with a higher likelihood of churn.

🔹 Analysis of healthcare utilization patterns shows that churned patients are distributed across various visit frequencies, with the highest churn observed among patients who made approximately **7 visits during the previous year**. This indicates that patient engagement levels may influence retention outcomes.

🔹 Churn rates vary across tenure groups, with the **25–36 month tenure group showing the highest churn rate (29%)**, followed by the **37–48 month group (26%)**. Combined with an average bill amount of **24.44K**, the findings suggest that improving satisfaction and retaining mid-tenure patients can significantly enhance long-term patient value and reduce churn.
